1 · The open

~90 sec

Say this before you touch the keyboard.

"So — quick context before I show you this. Right now, if someone on our team wants to know whether an internal API or a partner service is actually up, there's no real way to check besides manually pinging it, or waiting until a customer complains. There's no shared history, no dashboard, nothing that tells you an API's been degrading for the last three days.

That's the gap this closes. It's a synthetic monitoring platform — you register an endpoint once, and from that point on it gets health-checked automatically, forever, on whatever schedule you set. Could be every minute, could be every hour. And everything it finds — uptime, response time, failures — lands on one dashboard anyone on the team can pull up.

And deployment's deliberately boring — the entire thing, frontend and backend included, is four Docker containers brought up with one command. Nothing external, nothing to license, nothing that needs its own infra ticket. It's meant for whoever's closest to the pain: DevOps first, but developers and QA get the same visibility without having to ask.

Let me just show you, it's faster than explaining it."

2 · Show it live

~7 min

One continuous flow — each beat leads into the next, don't pause for permission.

01

Bring up the whole stack

terminal → docker compose up -d --build "Before anything else — this is the entire deployment." (run it, let all four containers come up) "Postgres, Redis, the API, and the frontend — four containers, one command. Nothing else to install anywhere, on any machine that has Docker."

02

Land on the dashboard — it's already working

open localhost:8088 "And here's the dashboard. I've already got a few real MTN endpoints registered — the MOMO API, our visitor management system, a couple more. This one's been running unattended, so this is genuinely what it looks like day to day, not a staged demo state."

"Notice the live beacon in the top right — point to the 'Live • auto-refresh in 15s' pill — the dashboard continuously updates itself every 15 seconds, and there's a 'Run all checks now' button if we ever want to force an immediate run across everything after a deploy."

"Top cards give executive KPIs at a glance: total endpoints, healthy, failed, and global average latency. Directly underneath is a Quick Actions bar with one-click shortcuts to Add Endpoint, Import OpenAPI, and SLA Reports."

"Right in the main view, we pair the macro and micro: on the left is the Health Distribution donut. On the right — point to Endpoint Health — is our worst-first live health list. We brought this right to the top so whatever is failing commands attention immediately. MOMO sandbox has actually been down for hours, and it floats right to the top with its exact downtime streak. We also have live filter chips — click 'Issues' — to isolate degraded APIs, and a search input for filtering when monitoring dozens of services."

"And down here — scroll to System Performance Trends — is our 24-hour aggregate trendline. I can flip between Latency (ms) and Availability (%), or toggle between 24 hours, 7 days, and 30 days to see if system performance is drifting."

"At the bottom — scroll to Recent Failures — is the full-width audit log showing every failure, status code, error, and our automated root-cause classification."

03

Add one more, live

Endpoints → Add Endpoint "Let's add one more so you see the onboarding step itself." (fill in name, URL, interval, hit Save) "Name, URL, how often to check it, what a healthy response looks like. Save — that's the entire flow. No code, no config file, no deploy."

04

Trigger a check

"It'll check on its own in a minute, but let's not wait — I'll just trigger it." click the ↻ next to the row "And there — status, response time, last checked, all live. In real use that just happens on its own, every minute, for every endpoint you've registered. I'm only doing it by hand so we don't sit here waiting."

05

Open the endpoint's detail page

click into the endpoint "This is where it gets useful over time — every single check ever run against this endpoint gets stored, so 'is it slow' or 'was it down last night' stops being a guess and becomes a graph you can just look at." (point at the response-time and availability charts, flick the 24h/7d/30d toggle once)

06

Stress it on demand

scroll to Load Test → set concurrency/requests → Run Load Test "One more thing on this page, and it's a direct answer to something you asked for — a performance testing engine, not just a ping every minute. I can fire a burst of concurrent requests at this endpoint right now." (run it, point at the results as they land) "Latency percentiles — p50, p95, p99 — not just an average, plus throughput and error rate under load. Same monitoring engine, just triggered on demand instead of on a schedule. And it's kept out of the uptime numbers on purpose — a load-test burst shouldn't make an endpoint look flakier than it actually is day to day."

07

Show it at scale

Import "Now, one endpoint's easy. What about a team with forty microservices? Two ways in — a CSV if you've already got a list, or this: point it at any team's OpenAPI spec, hit Discover, and every route in it shows up as a checkbox." (paste a Swagger URL, click Discover, let the list populate) "Nobody's hand-typing forty rows into a form."

08

Back to the dashboard — the new service is live

Dashboard "And now we're back on the dashboard. The new endpoints from the OpenAPI discovery are already incorporated into our health distribution, our trends chart, and our Endpoint Health table. Everything stays synchronized in real time without refreshing the browser."

09

Examine Reports & Root-Cause Classification

Reports "Next stop is Reports — three operational tiers. Daily numbers at the top. Then weekly — fastest, slowest, least stable, best availability, four rankings side by side. Then a 30-day rollup: how many services we're tracking, average uptime across all of them, how many separate incidents happened, how many are degraded right now, plus an uptime ranking."

(scroll to the bottom table) "And down here — every outage today, service by service, with the actual error. And next to it — this is root-cause analysis." (point at the Likely Cause column) "Every failure gets auto-classified — timeout, DNS issue, connection refused, server error, client error — instead of someone reading a raw error string and guessing what it means. It's rule-based against the error patterns we already capture, not a model, so I can tell you exactly why it called each one. This is the page I'd bring to a postmortem."

10

Ask the assistant — interactive ops chatbot

click floating chat icon (bottom-right) "And finally — this directly answers the last piece of your feedback: 'Add chat bots/AI agents'. We built an operational assistant right into the bottom corner of every page."

click 'What's down?' suggestion pill "You don't even have to hunt through dashboards. Ask 'What's down?', 'Slowest APIs', or 'Outages today' — and it immediately responds with live data, uptime streaks, and direct clickable links to the failing services."

"And I want to be 100% transparent about how it works: right now, without corporate approval for external LLM APIs, this is an honest, keyword-matched engine running directly against our PostgreSQL database and root-cause classifier. No hallucinatory answers, no external data leakage. And when LLM/agent access is approved in Phase 2, this exact UI widget connects directly to our model."

3 · Under the hood

~2 min

"Quick word on how it's actually built, for anyone curious —"

React + Vite UI → Express API → PostgreSQL ↺ node-cron scheduler → any HTTP endpoint
LayerTechWhy this, not the alternative
Frontend ReactTypeScriptTailwind v4Recharts Every chart on screen is real data rendered live — nothing's a screenshot or a mock number.
Data fetching React Query Dashboard and endpoint list refresh themselves every 15 seconds with live countdown indicator, no polling code to maintain.
Backend ExpressTypeScriptZod Every request is schema-validated before it touches the database — bad input never gets stored.
Scheduler node-cron Ticks every minute, asks "who's due," fires checks in parallel — same loop for 1 endpoint or 500.
Root Cause Analyzer Regex & Status Mapping Deterministic classification engine (rootCause.service.ts) mapping errors to Timeout, DNS, Refusal, Reset, 5xx, or 4xx.
Load Testing Engine Async Worker Pool On-demand concurrent stress-tester (loadTest.service.ts) generating p50, p95, p99 percentiles without skewing SLA metrics.
Chatbot Assistant Keyword & Intent Router Rule-based operations assistant (chat.service.ts + ChatWidget.tsx) querying monitoring telemetry with zero LLM dependency.
Database PostgreSQL Every check ever run is a row — that history is the whole reason the charts and reports exist.
Cache Redis Dashboard summary is cached 30 seconds so a room full of people refreshing doesn't hammer Postgres.
Infra Docker Composenginx That docker compose up -d --build from the top of the demo — nginx serves the built frontend and reverse-proxies /api straight to the backend container, same command on any internal server.

~1 min

"This first version delivered the core monitoring, load testing, root-cause classification, and chat assistant. Here is our phased Phase 2 AIOps roadmap directly addressing our operational goals."

PhaseCapabilityStatus / Implementation Path
Phase 1 API Performance Engine & Bulk Testing Built — Integrated concurrent load testing with p50/p95/p99 percentiles & OpenAPI bulk discovery.
Phase 1 Automated Root-Cause Classification Built — Pattern-based failure classifier on Dashboard & Daily Reports (Timeout, DNS, 5xx, 4xx).
Phase 1 Ops Chat Assistant (Rule-Based) Built — Floating interactive chat widget on all pages querying live status, outages, and rankings without external LLM dependencies.
Phase 2.1 Proactive Anomaly & Degradation Detection Next — Statistical baseline tracking (moving average + std dev) to alert on latency creep before an outage occurs.
Phase 2.2 Multi-Channel Alerting & Incident Grouping Next — Webhook integration (Slack / Teams / Email) with deduplication so 10 failed pings = 1 incident.
Phase 2.3 Human-in-the-Loop Auto-Remediation Roadmap — Automated remediation proposals with one-click approval buttons (e.g. restart pod, flush cache).
Phase 2.4 Pod & Microservice Observability Roadmap — Direct OpenShift & Kubernetes cluster metrics integration once cluster access is provisioned.
Phase 2.5 LLM-Powered Agentic Assistant Roadmap — Connect existing chat widget to approved internal LLM for freeform root-cause summaries and remediation dialogs.

5 · If they ask

buffer

"Does this hold up past a handful of endpoints?"

"Yes — the scheduler checks every due endpoint in parallel each tick, and the OpenAPI importer just did forty in one click a minute ago."

"What happens when something actually goes down?"

"It's on the dashboard immediately — Recent Failures and the health list both update within the minute. Alerting's item one on that roadmap I just showed — deliberately scoped out of this first version, not missed."

"Can it reach our internal services, not just public sites?"

"Anything reachable from wherever we deploy it — internal network included. That's the point of running it ourselves instead of a third-party monitor."

"Is the root-cause thing actually AI?"

"Not yet, and I'd rather be straight about that — it's pattern-matching against the error strings we already capture, not a model. It gets you from 'it's down' to 'why' today. Actual anomaly detection — catching a slow decline before it becomes an outage, not just classifying it after — is the logical next step, and it's on the roadmap I put together for exactly the kind of platform you described."

Land on: "Add an API once, and you're tracking it forever." Then stop talking.
